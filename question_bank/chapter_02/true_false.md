## Answer Key

| Question ID | Correct Answer | Justification |
|-------------|:--------------:|---------------|
| **CH02-TF-001** | **(B) False** | Kharanakhara was a lion, not a tiger. |
| **CH02-TF-002** | **(A) True** | Textbook explicitly states: 'Kharanakhara was a lion who ruled over a certain part of a jungle.' |
| **CH02-TF-003** | **(A) True** | The lion thought: 'It is for sure that some animal must be living in this big cave.' |
| **CH02-TF-004** | **(B) False** | He noticed the footprints of the lion leading into the cave. |
| **CH02-TF-005** | **(A) True** | He saw footprints entering but could not find any coming out. |
| **CH02-TF-006** | **(B) False** | Rocks and caves are non-living structures and cannot naturally talk. |
| **CH02-TF-007** | **(A) True** | Shouting at the cave was the jackal's clever plan to test the lion. |
| **CH02-TF-008** | **(A) True** | The lion foolishly answered on behalf of the cave to entice the jackal inside. |
| **CH02-TF-009** | **(B) False** | The jackal immediately ran away to save his life. |
| **CH02-TF-010** | **(A) True** | The story states: 'He ran off, as fast as he could and saved himself.' |
| **CH02-TF-011** | **(A) True** | The story concludes with: 'Presence of mind can save you from danger.' |
| **CH02-TF-012** | **(B) False** | It was sunset when the lion arrived at the cave. |
| **CH02-TF-013** | **(B) False** | Uninhabited means 'with nobody living in it'. |
| **CH02-TF-014** | **(B) False** | Echoes bounce off cave walls and amplify sounds, making them louder and frightening. |
| **CH02-TF-015** | **(B) False** | The jackal was clever and wise, whereas the lion made a foolish mistake. |
| **CH02-TF-016** | **(A) True** | Kharanakhara is a proper noun (specific name of an animal). |
| **CH02-TF-017** | **(B) False** | The lion was very hungry after wandering all day without prey. |
| **CH02-TF-018** | **(A) True** | Quick observation and clever testing under threat display presence of mind. |
| **CH02-TF-019** | **(A) True** | Panchatantra tales are ancient Indian fables featuring animal characters with morals. |
| **CH02-TF-020** | **(B) False** | The lion tried to trick him by saying: 'Hello Jackal, you may enter. It is safe inside.' |
| **CH02-TF-021** | **(A) True** | This is the exact direct speech spoken by the lion. |
| **CH02-TF-022** | **(A) True** | If the lion had kept silent, the jackal wouldn't have received immediate verbal proof. |
| **CH02-TF-023** | **(A) True** | 'Bound' in word meanings is defined as 'certain to do something'. |
| **CH02-TF-024** | **(B) False** | The jackal simply called out verbal questions to the cave. |
| **CH02-TF-025** | **(A) True** | The clever jackal saved himself from a powerful lion through wit rather than force. |
