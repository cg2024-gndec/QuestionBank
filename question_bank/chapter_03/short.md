## Answer Key & Model Answers

| Question ID | Model Answer (Class IV Standard) |
|-------------|-----------------------------------|
| **CH03-SA-001** | The king's favorite pet was a monkey. The king believed he was the most beautiful and intelligent monkey on the planet. |
| **CH03-SA-002** | In reality, the monkey was extremely foolish, quite contrary to what the king believed. |
| **CH03-SA-003** | The pet monkey was treated royally, moved freely across the palace, and was allowed into the king's private chambers. |
| **CH03-SA-004** | One afternoon, the king was sleeping peacefully in his personal room while the monkey kept watch. |
| **CH03-SA-005** | A fly entered the room and landed repeatedly on the king's chest while he was asleep. |
| **CH03-SA-006** | At first, the monkey waved his arms and flicked the fly away, but the fly kept coming back to land on the king. |
| **CH03-SA-007** | He got angry and excited because despite trying repeatedly, the fly refused to stay away from the king's chest. |
| **CH03-SA-008** | The monkey foolishly picked up a sharp sword to chase and strike the fly. |
| **CH03-SA-009** | The fly flew away unharmed, but the sword struck the king and severely wounded him. |
| **CH03-SA-010** | Beware of a foolish friend, for he can cause more harm than your enemy. |
| **CH03-SA-011** | 'Contrary' means opposite. *Sentence: Her opinion was contrary to what everyone expected.* |
| **CH03-SA-012** | 'Chambers' means a closed private room or apartment inside a palace. |
| **CH03-SA-013** | 'Flick' means to make something move with a sudden quick movement. *Sentence: He flicked the dust off his coat.* |
| **CH03-SA-014** | A foolish monkey lacks judgment and understanding of danger, so allowing him access to deadly weapons puts everyone's life at risk. |
| **CH03-SA-015** | *The fly flew away and the monkey got angry.* |
| **CH03-SA-016** | 'Oblivious' means the king was completely unaware and unmindful of the fly and monkey around him while he slept. |
| **CH03-SA-017** | No, the monkey did not intend to harm the king. He wanted to kill the annoying fly, but his foolish method caused severe harm. |
| **CH03-SA-018** | Panchatantra is an ancient collection of Indian animal fables that teach practical wisdom, morals, and smart living. |
| **CH03-SA-019** | (a) **Wise** / Intelligent  
(b) **Mildly** / Slightly |
| **CH03-SA-020** | A wise enemy can be anticipated with caution, but a foolish friend makes unpredictable, disastrous mistakes out of ignorance. |
| **CH03-SA-021** | Servants felt envious because the monkey enjoyed royal luxury and entry into private chambers forbidden even to trusted servants. |
| **CH03-SA-022** | **foolish** and **small**. |
| **CH03-SA-023** | He should have continued waving his hands, used a cloth, or gently woken up the king instead of grabbing a dangerous weapon. |
| **CH03-SA-024** | Being 'slightly hurt' means minor scratches, whereas being 'severely wounded' means suffering deep, serious, and painful bodily injury. |
| **CH03-SA-025** | A king loved his foolish pet monkey and allowed him into his private room. While guarding the sleeping king, the monkey struck a fly with a sword, missing the fly and severely wounding the king. |
