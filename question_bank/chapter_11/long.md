| **CH11-LA-001** | The lifecycle has four stages: 1. **Egg**: Female frogs lay jelly-like eggs in water (*spawning*), taking 1-3 weeks to hatch. 2. **Tadpole**: Hatches with a tail and no legs, living in water and breathing through gills. 3. **Froglet**: Grows hind and front legs for hopping, develops lungs to breathe air, and has a shrinking tail. 4. **Adult Frog**: Fully grown with no tail, living on land and in water. The female lays eggs to start a new cycle. | Stage 1 & 2: 2 Marks<br>Stage 3 & 4: 2 Marks<br>Cycle continuity: 1 Mark |
| **CH11-LA-002** | Metamorphosis is the process of changing form as an organism grows. It is crucial because it allows the frog to transition from an aquatic herbivore (tadpole breathing with gills in water) into a terrestrial carnivore (adult frog breathing with lungs on land), reducing competition for food between young and adults. | Metamorphosis definition: 2 Marks<br>Biological adaptation value: 3 Marks |
| **CH11-LA-003** | In Stage 2 (Tadpole), the organism lives underwater and extracts dissolved oxygen using feather-like **gills**. In Stage 3 (Froglet) and Stage 4 (Adult Frog), internal **lungs** develop, allowing the frog to take in atmospheric oxygen from air when it moves out of water onto land. | Gills in tadpole: 2 Marks<br>Lungs in froglet/adult: 2 Marks<br>Transition rationale: 1 Mark |
| **CH11-LA-004** | A tadpole has a long, flexible tail for swimming in water and lacks legs.
