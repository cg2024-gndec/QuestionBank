## Answer Key & Model Answers

| Question ID | Model Answer (Class IV Standard) |
|-------------|-----------------------------------|
| **CH08-SA-001** | Bhagat Singh was born on September 28, 1907 in Punjab to Sardar Kishan Singh and Vidyawati. |
| **CH08-SA-002** | His goal was to drive the British out of India. He hailed from a patriotic family of revolutionaries actively involved in the independence struggle. |
| **CH08-SA-003** | At age 12, the brutal killing of innocent Indians at Jallianwala Bagh deeply disturbed him and filled his heart with a strong desire for freedom. |
| **CH08-SA-004** | He brought home a bottle of blood-soaked mud to keep with him as a constant reminder of British cruelty and his vow to liberate India. |
| **CH08-SA-005** | He supported the Swadeshi movement by burning foreign-made goods, sacrificing luxury clothes, and wearing only Indian khadi cloth. |
| **CH08-SA-006** | Seeing continuous British atrocities and violence against peaceful protesters like Lala Lajpat Rai made him believe that force was needed to overthrow British rule. |
| **CH08-SA-007** | He joined National College, where he was deeply influenced by the great patriot Lala Lajpat Rai. |
| **CH08-SA-008** | Lala Lajpat Rai was beaten to death in a police lathi charge. To avenge his mentor's death, Bhagat Singh killed British officer General Saunders. |
| **CH08-SA-009** | He was hanged to death on March 23, 1931 along with his revolutionary friends Rajguru and Sukhdev. |
| **CH08-SA-010** | In jail, he read widely and wrote extensive articles about colonial oppression, freedom, and the path of revolution. |
| **CH08-SA-011** | A 'revolutionary' is someone who fights for radical political change using new ideas; a 'massacre' is the killing of a large number of helpless people. |
| **CH08-SA-012** | 'Atrocities' means extremely cruel actions; a 'martyr' is a person who sacrifices his life for a noble cause or principle. |
| **CH08-SA-013** | 'Shaheed' means martyr. The title honors his supreme sacrifice of giving his life at a young age for India's freedom. |
| **CH08-SA-014** | *General Saunders was killed by Bhagat Singh to avenge Lala Lajpat Rai's death.* |
| **CH08-SA-015** | *Bhagat Singh inspired the youth of India with his bravery.* |
| **CH08-SA-016** | **Proper Nouns**: Shaheed Bhagat Singh, National College, Punjab.<br>**Common Nouns**: college, martyr. |
| **CH08-SA-017** | He felt that winning freedom for his country was a higher responsibility than personal career goals or formal education. |
| **CH08-SA-018** | It damaged British economic interests in India and demonstrated self-reliance and national unity through native cloth (*khadi*). |
| **CH08-SA-019** | (a) **Oppression**: Freedom / Liberty  
(b) **Cruel (Atrocities)**: Kind / Merciful |
| **CH08-SA-020** | *On March 23, 1931, Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev were hanged in Lahore.* |
| **CH08-SA-021** | He wanted to understand different historical strategies of overthrowing tyrannical rulers and learn effective revolutionary methods. |
| **CH08-SA-022** | After day classes, he met his fellow patriot friends in the evening to plan and discuss strategies for driving out the British. |
| **CH08-SA-023** | It served as a permanent symbol of the martyrs' sacrifice, keeping his passion for India's independence burning every single day. |
| **CH08-SA-024** | (a) Young, **Younger**, Youngest  
(b) Great, **Greater**, Greatest |
