## Answer Key & Model Answers

| Question ID | Model Answer (Class IV Standard) |
|-------------|-----------------------------------|
| **CH09-SA-001** | Garba is a lively, colourful folk dance originating from the western state of Gujarat in India. |
| **CH09-SA-002** | 'Garba' comes from the Sanskrit word *Garbha*, meaning 'womb'. It symbolizes life, creation, and divine energy. |
| **CH09-SA-003** | It combines artistic dance movements with sacred prayers and devotional singing in honor of the divine. |
| **CH09-SA-004** | It was initially performed to pay homage to Goddess Durga during the 9-night Navratri festival. |
| **CH09-SA-005** | An earthen pot with holes called a *garbo*, filled with water and decked with a lighted lamp (*diya*), is placed in the center. |
| **CH09-SA-006** | Garba is mainly performed during Navratri, a festival consisting of nine nights dedicated to Goddess Durga. |
| **CH09-SA-007** | Women wear colourful embroidered *chaniya choli* with traditional jewellery, while men wear *kediyu* (frock-style tops) and *dhotis*. |
| **CH09-SA-008** | Dhol, tabla, and harmonium are traditional instruments accompanying Garba. |
| **CH09-SA-009** | Over time, Garba evolved from temple rituals into a popular celebration dance performed at weddings, social events, and community halls. |
| **CH09-SA-010** | People perform Garba in open grounds, community centers, school grounds, and decorated festival venues. |
| **CH09-SA-011** | 'Womb' is the body part where a baby grows before birth; 'inherent' means an unremovable basic feature. |
| **CH09-SA-012** | 'Emanate' means to originate or flow out from. Melodious Garba music emanates from instruments like the dhol and harmonium. |
| **CH09-SA-013** | The light (*deepak*) inside the perforated pot represents the divine soul glowing within the human body. |
| **CH09-SA-014** | *Devotional songs are sung by people during Garba.* |
| **CH09-SA-015** | *Garba combined elements of dance and prayers.* |
| **CH09-SA-016** | **Proper Nouns**: Gujarat, Goddess Durga.<br>**Common Nouns**: dance, pot. |
| **CH09-SA-017** | People of all ages and backgrounds join hands in circles, dancing together to rhythm, creating joy and community unity. |
| **CH09-SA-018** | Folk dances preserve our ancient heritage, showcase traditional attire and music, and keep cultural identity alive. |
| **CH09-SA-019** | (a) **Ancient**: Modern / Recent  
(b) **Devotional**: Secular / Non-religious |
| **CH09-SA-020** | *During Navratri, people in Gujarat perform Garba around an earthen pot.* |
| **CH09-SA-021** | Dancing in a circle represents the cycle of life revolving continuously around the central divine light. |
| **CH09-SA-022** | A *kediyu* is a short, pleated frock-style top worn by men during traditional Garba performances. |
| **CH09-SA-023** | Songs glorify the power and grace of Goddess Durga, celebrate feminine strength, and praise divine creation. |
| **CH09-SA-024** | (a) Lively, **Livelier**, Liveliest  
(b) Popular, **More Popular**, Most Popular |
| **CH09-SA-025** | Garba is an ancient Gujarati folk dance performed in circles around a lighted garbo pot to honor Goddess Durga during Navratri. Today, it brings communities together in colorful, joyful celebration across India and the world. |
