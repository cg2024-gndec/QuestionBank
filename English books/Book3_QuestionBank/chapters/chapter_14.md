Talking Tree
—Anonymous
1
The
Deep within the
ancient wood,
An old, wise talking tree stood,
Its bark was rugged, roots so deep,
Its branches cradled birds in sleep.
One day a child wandered near,
Eyes wide open, full of cheer,
She touched the bark and heard a sigh,
A whisper soft, like a lullaby.
"Who speaks?" she asked in surprise,
The tree blinked its ancient eyes,
"I am the keeper of tales untold,
Of sunlit dawns and nights so cold."
It spoke of kings and battles fought,
Of sailors lost and treasures sought,
Of rivers carving rocks with might,
Of stars that gleamed in velvet night.
It told of flowers that bloomed and died,
Of autumn winds and spring's first tide,
Of nests built high on swaying boughs,
Of lovers making heartfelt vows.

The child listened with rapt delight,
Through day and into the falling night,
She learned of life, of joy and pain,
Of sunshine and of drizzling rain.
At last, the tree grew tired and still,
The moon rose high behind the hill,
"Come again," it softly said,
Before it rested its sleepy head.
With a smile and a heart so full,
The child ran home under the moon's pull,
Knowing sheld found a friend so grand,
The talking tree of the enchanted land.
