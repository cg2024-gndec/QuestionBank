Fountain Pen
Around 3000 BCE, Sumerians used reed styluses to make
marks on clay tablets. Egyptians developed ink and brushes made
from reeds to write on papyrus scrolls some time later. Quill pens,
made from bird feathers, made an appearance around 6th century.
For many centuries, people continued to write with quills or reed pens.
But they made writing a cumbersome and time-consuming task. The
world of writing was eventually revolutionised in the early 19th century.
In the year 1827, Romanian inventor Petrache Poenaru came up with a
new design of a pen. His design featured an ink reservoir that allowed
continuous writing making a significant improvement over dip pens.
He was the first one to be granted patent for such a design.
Later, in the 1880s, American inventor Lewis Waterman improved
the design by introducing a capillary feed system, which regulated
ink flow and prevented leaks. Waterman's improvisations made
fountain pens more reliable and popular for everyday writing.
