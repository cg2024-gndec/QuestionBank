The Brahmaputra River:
A Lifeline of South Asia
The Brahmaputra river is a major
river in South Asia. It flows
through China, India and
Bangladesh before emptying into
the Bay of Bengal. Originating
from the Angsi glacier in Tibet,
YarlungTsangpo flows through
Tibet and covers about 1,625
kilometres. It takes a dramatic
southward bend at the eastern
end of the Himalayas and enters
India in Arunachal Pradesh. Here
it is called the Siang or Dihang.
When it flows through Assam it is
known as the Brahmaputra and is
joined by several
tributaries here
like Dibang, Lohit etc.
After flowing through Assam it enters Bangladesh where it is known as the
Jamuna. In Bangladesh it merges with the Ganga (Padma) and the Meghna rivers,
orms a huge delta and empties into the Bay of Bengal.
Historically, this river has served as a crucial trade route by connecting Tibet
with Assam and Bengal. This river also helped in the spread of Buddhism. The
British used this river for transporting a number of goods which resulted in the
economic development of the region.
