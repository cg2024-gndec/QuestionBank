- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Easy

**Question**: Examine the fantasy landscape and sensory imagery described in 'My Dream Adventure'.

- **Answer Key**: The poem 'My Dream Adventure' creates a delightful, whimsical candy land through rich sensory imagery:
1. **Chocolate Stream**: The speaker begins the adventure sailing along a river made of liquid chocolate.
2. **Candy-Cane Trees**: The trees are tall and colorful, explicitly compared to peppermint candy canes using a simile.
3. **Marshmallow Clouds & Jellybean Rains**: The weather features soft, fluffy marshmallow clouds dropping sweet, colorful jellybeans as rain.
4. **Gold & Red Mountains**: Mountains glow in brilliant gold and red colors, with a rocky bed made of crunchy cookie crumbs.
5. **Cupcake Streets**: The village streets are neatly lined with sweet cupcakes while birds sing melodious songs.
This combination of visual colors, sweet tastes, and soft textures creates a comforting fantasy realm for young readers.

### Question 2
- **Question ID**: BK05_CH13_LA_002
- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Bloom Level**: Understanding
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Describe the encounter with the dragon and explain how it subverts traditional dragon stories.

- **Answer Key**: In traditional fairy tales, dragons are fierce, fire-breathing monsters that terrorize villages and fight knights. 'My Dream Adventure' subverts this scary trope completely:
- The speaker meets a dragon described as 'green and bright'.
- Instead of roaring or attacking, the dragon 'just smiled, not there to fight'.
- The dragon warmly invites the speaker: 'Come ride with me!'
- Together, they fly high overhead, soaring above gold and red mountains and cupcake streets.
This portrayal transforms a potential monster into a friendly guide and flying companion, teaching children that unfamiliar things are not always scary.

### Question 3
- **Question ID**: BK05_CH13_LA_003
- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Bloom Level**: Understanding
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Examine the final stanza of the poem and explain the kite metaphor for building real-life courage.

- **Answer Key**: The final stanza transitions from night fantasy to a powerful moral message for real life:
> *'So, when you're scared to try and fly,
Remember the kite up in the sky,
Brave the wind and take the flight,
You'll find your wings and soar in light.'*
- **Kite Metaphor**: A kite cannot fly in still air; it needs opposing wind to gain altitude. The poet uses the kite to symbolize human courage.
- **Real-Life Meaning**: When children feel scared to try new, difficult challenges ('scared to try and fly'), they must face their fears ('brave the wind'). By taking the leap ('take the flight'), they discover their own inner abilities ('find your wings') and achieve success ('soar in light').

### Question 4
- **Question ID**: BK05_CH13_LA_004
- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Bloom Level**: Remembering
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Describe the structure, rhyme scheme, and poetic devices used in 'My Dream Adventure'.

- **Answer Key**: The poem 'My Dream Adventure' is crafted with engaging poetic elements:
1. **Structure**: It contains 5 stanzas of 4 lines each (quatrains), totaling 20 lines.
2. **Rhyme Scheme**: Every stanza follows an **AABB** rhyme scheme (rhyming couplets):
   - Stanza 1: dream/stream (A), canes/rains (B)
   - Stanza 2: bright/fight (A), said/overhead (B)
   - Stanza 3: red/bed (A), sweet/street (B)
   - Stanza 4: rise/eyes (A), light/night (B)
   - Stanza 5: fly/sky (A), flight/light (B)
3. **Poetic Devices**:
   - **Simile**: 'trees were tall, like candy canes'.
   - **Metaphor**: 'cookie crumbs as rocky bed', kite metaphor for courage, 'find your wings'.
