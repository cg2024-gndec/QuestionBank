### Question 1
- **Question ID**: BK05_CH15_LA_001
- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Bloom Level**: Remembering
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Examine the format and structural rules of Formal Letter Writing as presented in Chapter 15.

- **Answer Key**: Formal Letter Writing follows strict layout rules to maintain professional clarity and respect:
1. **Sender's Address & Date**: Placed at the top left corner.
2. **Receiver's Designation & Address**: The official title and address of the recipient (e.g., 'The Principal, ABC School, City').
3. **Subject Line**: A concise one-sentence statement declaring the exact purpose of the letter (e.g., 'Subject: Request for Leave').
4. **Salutation**: Formal greeting such as 'Respected Sir/Madam'.
5. **Body Text**: Divided logically into paragraphs—stating the student's name/class, clear reason for request/complaint, dates, and assurance to complete missed work.
6. **Subscription & Signature**: Professional closing ('Thanking you', 'Yours sincerely/faithfully') followed by the sender's full name and class.

### Question 3

- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Bloom Level**: Remembering
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Examine the format, purpose, and key elements of a Diary Entry as presented in Chapter 15.

- **Answer Key**: A Diary Entry is a private personal record of thoughts, feelings, and events:
1. **Header Details**: Opens with Date, Day, and Time (e.g., '9:30 PM') at the top left.
2. **Salutation**: Traditional opening 'Dear Diary'.
3. **First-Person Perspective**: Written in the first person ('I', 'me', 'my') to record daily activities and personal experiences.
4. **Emotional Reflection**: Expresses honest feelings ('I felt excited/surprised/sad because...') and reflects on the day's highlights or funny incidents.
5. **Sign-off**: Closes with a reflective evening thought ('It was truly a wonderful day') followed by 'Good Night, [Your Name]'.

### Question 4
- **Question ID**: BK05_CH15_LA_004
- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Bloom Level**: Comparing
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Compare Formal Letter Writing with Informal Letter Writing across format, tone, and audience.

- **Answer Key**: Formal and Informal letters differ significantly across three dimensions:
- **Audience**: Formal letters are sent to school principals, postmasters, librarians, and municipal officers; Informal letters are sent to friends, cousins, parents, and grandparents.
- **Format**: Formal letters include receiver address and a mandatory Subject line; Informal letters omit receiver address and Subject line.
- **Tone & Diction**: Formal letters use polite, objective, administrative language ('request', 'grant permission', 'Yours sincerely'); Informal letters use warm, conversational, affectionate language ('awesome', 'cant wait to hear', 'Your friend').

### Question 5
- **Question ID**: BK05_CH15_LA_005
- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Bloom Level**: Understanding
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Describe the structure of an Essay and outline the points for 'The Importance of Trees in Our Life'.

- **Answer Key**: An Essay is structured into three main parts:
1. **Introduction**: Introduces the topic ('Trees are green gold and essential for all living beings').
2. **Body Paragraphs**: Explains key arguments in detail:
   - *Environmental Benefits*: Oxygen production, absorbing carbon dioxide, supporting rain, preventing soil erosion.
   - *Human Utilities*: Providing fruits, shade, timber, rubber, paper, and medicinal herbs.
   - *Ecosystem Support*: Harboring birds, animals, and insects.
3. **Conclusion**: Summarizes the main theme and delivers a call to action ('We must protect forests and plant more trees for a green future').

**Question**: Describe the structure of a Story composition and outline the plot for 'The Magical Pen'.
