### Question 1
- **Question ID**: CH01-LA-001
- **Type**: Long Answer
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Bloom Level**: Understanding
- **Topic**: Story Summary
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Write a summary of the story "Empty Pot" in your own words.

- **Answer Key**: Once upon a time in China, a boy named Ping loved plants and gardening. The old Emperor had no children, so he wanted to find a successor. He gave each child a seed and asked them to grow it and return after six months. Ping tried his best — he changed the soil, watered the seed regularly, and put it in the sun — but nothing grew. On the day of return, all other children had beautiful plants, but Ping came with an empty pot. The Emperor revealed that all seeds were boiled and could not grow. The children with plants had cheated. Ping was the only honest child, and the Emperor chose him as the next king. The story teaches us that honesty is the best policy.

**Question**: Explain why the Emperor's plan to give boiled seeds was clever. How did it help him find the right successor?

- **Answer Key**: The Emperor's plan was extremely clever for several reasons. First, boiled seeds cannot grow into plants — this is a scientific fact. So, anyone who came back with a flowering plant must have cheated by replacing the seed. Second, the test was simple — every child received the same seed, so there was no advantage given to anyone. Third, it tested the most important quality for a king — honesty. A dishonest king cannot be trusted by his people. Fourth, the test also revealed which parents taught good values. Only Ping's parents encouraged honesty. Finally, the plan was effective because it required no special knowledge — just basic integrity. The Emperor found exactly what he was looking for: an honest child worthy of ruling the kingdom.

- **Topic**: Science Connection
- **Marks**: 5

**Question**: Why can boiled seeds not grow into plants? Explain the science behind it and its role in the story.

- **Answer Key**: Seeds contain tiny living parts called embryos that have the potential to grow into new plants. When seeds are boiled, the extreme heat kills these living cells inside the seed. The proteins in the seed get damaged, and the enzymes needed for germination are destroyed. Once the embryo is dead, the seed can never germinate, no matter how much care is given — water, sunlight, and good soil cannot bring dead cells back to life.

In the story, the Emperor used this scientific fact cleverly. He knew that boiled seeds would never grow, so any child returning with a plant must have cheated. This made the test foolproof — there was no chance that an honest child could have a growing plant. Ping's gardening skills were excellent, but they were useless against dead seeds. The Emperor's plan worked perfectly because it relied on this unchangeable fact of nature to separate the honest children from the dishonest ones.
